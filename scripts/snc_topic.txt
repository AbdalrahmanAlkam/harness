Counterexample search for Seymour's Second Neighborhood Conjecture.

Let D = (V, E) be a finite simple digraph with no directed 2-cycles: for all u, v
in V, (u, v) in E implies (v, u) not in E. For a vertex v define the first
out-neighbourhood N+(v) = {u in V : (v, u) in E} and the second out-neighbourhood
N++(v) = {w in V \ (N+(v) union {v}) : exists u in N+(v) with (u, w) in E}.
Seymour's Second Neighborhood Conjecture asserts that every such digraph has at
least one vertex v with |N++(v)| >= |N+(v)|.

This is an OPEN PROBLEM. Do not claim to resolve it. Build the claims array as a
ladder:

(a) One claim with kind "conjecture" stating the problem above in plain
    mathematics. It needs no sympy_expression. It is recorded as the target and
    is never marked proven.

(b) Decidable claims beneath it, each kind "theorem" with its own sympy_expression
    and lean_statement, in increasing difficulty:
      - exhaustive verification that the conjecture holds for every 2-cycle-free
        digraph on at most n vertices, for the largest n an exact enumeration can
        settle;
      - an exact procedure that, given a proposed adjacency matrix, recomputes
        |N+(v)| and |N++(v)| for every vertex and decides whether that matrix is a
        counterexample;
      - boundary and degenerate cases: n = 0, n = 1, no out-edges, tournaments,
        regular out-degree;
      - a formalisation of the conjecture in Lean over Finset, stated as a goal.

(c) An explicit statement of what remains open.

A candidate counterexample counts only if its neighbourhood counts are
recomputed from the adjacency matrix by exact code, not asserted.
